# Implement Intelligent Consultation Routing and Automated Care Workflow

You are a **senior healthtech software architect and full-stack engineer** working inside an **existing teleophthalmology application in Replit**.

The application already contains dashboards, authentication, patient records, clinician workflows, appointments, notifications, and a consultation/referral foundation.

Your task is to **enhance the existing application**, not rebuild it.

The goal is to replace the current **randomized “assign to any available specialist” workflow** with an **intelligent, automated consultation-routing system** that is seamless for patients, frontline clinicians, ophthalmologists, and administrators.

## Core Product Principle

The system should operate like this:

> **Users create consultation requests. The platform determines the appropriate care pathway and automatically connects the request to the most appropriate qualified available specialist.**

Randomized assignment may still be used, but **only as the final tie-breaker among equally eligible clinicians**.

Do not force users to manually select an ophthalmologist unless necessary.

# 7. Routing Engine

Create or enhance a dedicated routing service/module.

Conceptually:

```text
ConsultationRequest
        ↓
RoutingEngine
        ↓
RoutingDecision
```

The routing engine should determine:

1. required specialty
2. eligible clinicians
3. available clinicians
4. continuity preference
5. workload score
6. facility/geographic considerations
7. patient preference
8. final selected clinician
9. fallback strategy

# 8. Eligibility Filtering

Before scoring clinicians, eliminate anyone who should not receive the request.

A clinician is eligible only if:

* they are active
* they have the required clinical role
* they have the required specialty/capability
* they are authorized to treat the patient/case
* they are available under the relevant scheduling rules
* they have not exceeded configured workload limits
* they are not blocked/unavailable
* they are not already the wrong specialist for the case

Do not simply select from every ophthalmologist in the database.

---

# 9. Specialty Matching

Specialty matching should be configurable.

Example:

```text
Retina case
    ↓
Retina specialists
    ↓
If none available
    ↓
General ophthalmologist with retina capability
    ↓
If none available
    ↓
Regional / escalation queue
```

Do not hardcode a single specialty hierarchy into the UI.

The backend should own the routing rules.

---

# 10. Continuity of Care

This should have a high priority in assignment.

Before choosing a new clinician, check:

> Does this patient already have a relevant ophthalmologist?

For example:

```text
Patient
  ↓
Primary ophthalmologist = Dr. Sarah
  ↓
Follow-up consultation
```

Prefer Dr. Sarah if:

* she is still active
* she is clinically appropriate
* she is available within the required timeframe

This should be configurable.

Continuity should strongly influence assignment, especially for follow-ups.

---

# 11. Availability

Use the existing scheduling/availability system if one exists.

Do not create a duplicate availability table unnecessarily.

Consider:

* online/offline status
* appointment schedule
* working hours
* leave
* unavailable status
* current consultation
* maximum concurrent cases

For urgent cases, prioritize clinicians who are actively available now.

---

# 12. Workload Balancing

Among eligible clinicians, calculate workload.

Potential metrics:

* open consultation requests
* pending reviews
* active consultations
* urgent cases
* outstanding second opinions
* recent workload

Do not simply use total patient count.

A clinician with 10 routine cases may be less overloaded than someone with 5 urgent cases.

Create a configurable workload score.

---

# 13. Facility / Geographic Constraints

Use location/facility where relevant.

For remote consultation, geography should generally have lower weight.

For consultations that may require physical referral or in-person care, proximity may become more important.

Example:

```text
Rural patient
   ↓
Teleophthalmology review
   ↓
Remote specialist

If physical referral required
   ↓
Nearest appropriate referral facility
```

Do not let geography override urgent clinical need.

---

# 14. Patient Preference

Support patient preference when appropriate.

For example:

* preferred ophthalmologist
* preferred facility
* preferred appointment time

However:

> Patient preference must never force assignment to an unavailable or clinically inappropriate clinician.

The routing engine should treat preference as one scoring factor.

---

# 15. Assignment Algorithm

Implement a configurable weighted scoring model.

Start with a sensible default such as:

```text
Specialty Match         30%
Availability            25%
Continuity of Care      20%
Workload Balance        15%
Facility/Geography      5%
Patient Preference      5%
```

These values must be configurable.

Do not hardcode them throughout the application.

Create a central routing configuration.

Example:

```text
ROUTING_WEIGHTS = {
    specialty: 0.30,
    availability: 0.25,
    continuity: 0.20,
    workload: 0.15,
    geography: 0.05,
    preference: 0.05
}
```

---

# 16. Preserve Randomized Assignment — But Change Its Role

The existing randomized assignment behavior should **not simply be deleted**.

Use it as a tie-breaker.

Example:

```text
Eligible clinicians:

Dr. A = 87 points
Dr. B = 87 points
Dr. C = 86 points
Dr. D = 72 points

Select from:
Dr. A
Dr. B

Randomized tie-breaker
        ↓
Dr. B selected
```

This preserves fairness without making assignment arbitrary.

The randomization must occur **after eligibility and ranking**, never before.

# 20. Escalation System

Do not let important requests silently remain in queues.

Implement configurable Service-Level Agreements.

Example:

```text
Emergency
→ acknowledge within configured emergency SLA

Urgent
→ acknowledge within configured urgent SLA

Routine
→ acknowledge within configured routine SLA
```

When the SLA is exceeded:

```text
Assigned clinician
        ↓
No acknowledgement
        ↓
Escalation
        ↓
Next eligible specialist
```

If no specialist accepts:

```text
Escalation Queue
        ↓
Clinical Coordinator
```

Do not hardcode exact time limits unless existing product requirements already define them.

Make them configurable.
